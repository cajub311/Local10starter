# Minnesota & Federal Law Gap Analysis — Atlas / Local 10 CBA
### Bargaining-prep reference for the next contract — NOT a change to the current CBA

**Contract reviewed:** Atlas Manufacturing & IASMART Local 10, April 1, 2025 – March 31, 2027 (`contract.pdf`)
**Purpose:** Identify benefits and protections that Minnesota and federal law already provide, or make available to negotiate, that are **not** currently written into the contract.

> 🚫 **This document does not alter the contract.** The current CBA (`contract.pdf`) is unchanged and stays in force exactly as written until the Union and Company bargain a successor agreement. The "proposed language" below is **draft material for the next round of negotiations** (or a mid-term Letter of Understanding **only if both parties agree**) — nothing here is in effect now.
>
> 🟢 **Two kinds of items below:** (1) **rights members already have by law** even though they're absent from the CBA — members can use these today, and a steward can enforce them through DLI/agency channels; and (2) **bargaining goals** — improvements on top of the legal floor to win at the table.
>
> ⚠️ **Not legal advice.** This is a steward/rep working tool. Statute citations are current as of June 2026. Confirm exact text with the MN Revisor (revisor.mn.gov) and your Business Representative before bargaining or filing anything.

---

## How to read this

For each gap:
- **MN law** — the statute and what it requires/allows.
- **Contract today** — what (if anything) the current CBA says.
- **The gap / opportunity** — why it matters and what you can win.
- **Proposed language** — drop-in starting point for bargaining or an LOU.

Items are ordered roughly by impact.

## 1. Minnesota Paid Leave (state PFML) — HIGHEST PRIORITY ⭐

**MN law (Minn. Stat. ch. 268B):** Effective **January 1, 2026**, Minnesota Paid Leave provides eligible workers **up to 12 weeks of medical leave** and **up to 12 weeks of family leave**, capped at a combined **20 weeks** in a benefit year. It is funded by a **0.88% payroll premium** (2026 rate). The employer may deduct **no more than 50%** of the premium from the employee — meaning the **employer must pay at least half**, and may choose to pay more. Benefits are a percentage of wages paid by the state, not the employer.

**Contract today:** The CBA only contains **unpaid** FMLA (Section 5.F.5.a) and unpaid personal leave. There is **no mention of MN Paid Leave at all**, even though the program goes live in the middle of this contract term.

**The gap / opportunity:** This is the single biggest item. Because the law sets only a *floor*, the union can bargain real money and protections on top of the state program:

1. **Employer pays 100% of the premium** (law only requires 50%). This is a direct raise — it removes a payroll deduction from every member's check.
2. **Wage top-up / supplement** — Paid Leave replaces only a percentage of wages. Negotiate that members may use accrued Vacation/Personal/ESST to "top up" to 100% of normal pay, and/or that the Company supplements the difference for a set number of weeks.
3. **Job protection & reinstatement** beyond the statutory minimum — same job back, same shift, same classification.
4. **Continued seniority and benefit accrual** during Paid Leave (the current FMLA section *suspends* Vacation accrual — see Section 5.F.5.a "Use and Accrual" — don't let that carry over to Paid Leave).
5. **Continued health insurance at active-employee cost share** during Paid Leave (today, once FMLA goes unpaid the member must mail in full premiums — Section 5.F.5.a).
6. **No attendance points** for any Paid Leave absence (mirror the ESST carve-out in Section 5.E).

**Proposed language (new subsection under Section 5.F — Leaves of Absence):**

> **Minnesota Paid Leave.** Employees are eligible for benefits under the Minnesota Paid Leave program (Minn. Stat. ch. 268B) as provided by law. In addition:
> (a) The Company shall pay one hundred percent (100%) of the Paid Leave premium and shall make no payroll deduction from employees for the program.
> (b) An employee on approved Paid Leave may elect to use accrued Vacation, Personal Time, and/or ESST to supplement Paid Leave benefits up to one hundred percent (100%) of the employee's normal straight-time pay. Such use is at the employee's option and shall not be required.
> (c) Seniority shall continue to accrue and Vacation shall continue to accrue during Minnesota Paid Leave.
> (d) The Company shall maintain group health, dental, and other insurance during Paid Leave at the same cost-sharing level that applies to active employees, with the employee responsible only for the employee share through the normal payroll/payment process.
> (e) No attendance points (Section 5.E) shall be assessed for any absence covered by Minnesota Paid Leave.
> (f) Upon return from Paid Leave the employee shall be reinstated to the same position, shift, and classification held before the leave, consistent with Minn. Stat. ch. 268B.

## 2. Paid lactation breaks (current law is stronger than the contract)

**MN law (Minn. Stat. § 181.939):** As amended in 2023–2024, employers must provide **reasonable paid break time each day** for an employee to express milk, with **no 12-month time limit** and **no "undue disruption" exception**. Compensation may **not** be reduced for this time. A clean, private space (not a bathroom) near the work area must be provided.

**Contract today:** The only reference to nursing is in Section 1.B, which mentions "areas designated for expressing breast milk" — but solely in the context of **camera placement/privacy**, not as a paid-break right. There is **no lactation-break benefit** in the contract.

**The gap / opportunity:** Members are entitled to **paid** lactation breaks by law; putting it in the CBA makes it grievable rather than dependent on a DLI complaint.

**Proposed language (new item under Section 5.B — Rest Periods):**

> **Lactation Breaks.** The Company shall provide reasonable **paid** break time each day for an employee to express milk for the employee's child, consistent with Minn. Stat. § 181.939. These breaks are in addition to, and shall not reduce, the employee's regular paid break or pay. The Company shall provide a clean, private space, other than a restroom, in reasonable proximity to the work area, with access to an electrical outlet. Lactation-break time shall not be counted as an absence and shall not result in attendance points.

## 3. Pregnancy accommodations & unpaid pregnancy/parental leave (WESA)

**MN law:**
- **§ 181.9414 (pregnancy accommodations):** Employers with 15+ employees must provide, **on request and without a doctor's note**, accommodations including **more frequent/longer breaks, seating, a 20-lb lifting limit, and temporary transfer** to less strenuous/hazardous work. No "undue hardship" defense for the basic accommodations.
- **§ 181.941 (pregnancy & parental leave):** Up to **12 weeks** unpaid, job-protected leave for birth/adoption/prenatal care/pregnancy-related incapacity; health insurance continues during the leave. (This can run alongside / be coordinated with Paid Leave above.)

**Contract today:** No pregnancy-accommodation language. FMLA (Section 5.F.5.a) covers some parental leave but only for employees who meet FMLA's 12-month/1,250-hour/50-employee thresholds — **MN § 181.941 has lower thresholds**, so some members covered by state law are not covered by the contract's FMLA section.

**The gap / opportunity:** Guarantee accommodations and state-law parental leave to members who don't qualify for federal FMLA.

**Proposed language (new subsection under Section 5.F):**

> **Pregnancy Accommodations and Parental Leave.** Consistent with Minn. Stat. §§ 181.9414 and 181.941, a pregnant employee shall, upon request, receive reasonable accommodations including but not limited to more frequent or longer restroom, food, and water breaks; seating; and limits on lifting over twenty (20) pounds, without the need for medical documentation. The Company shall provide up to twelve (12) weeks of job-protected pregnancy and parental leave with continuation of group health coverage, regardless of whether the employee meets the eligibility thresholds of the federal FMLA. No attendance points shall be assessed for protected leave or accommodation time.

## 4. Drug & alcohol testing — DATWA employee protections

**MN law (Minn. Stat. §§ 181.950–181.957, "DATWA"):** Minnesota gives tested employees strong rights, most importantly: an employer **may not discharge an employee on a first positive confirmatory test** without **first offering the employee the chance to complete a counseling or rehabilitation program** (§ 181.953, subd. 10). DATWA also requires a written testing policy, confirmatory retesting before any action, the right to explain a positive result, and the right to request a confirmatory retest at the employee's own expense.

**Contract today:** Section 6.B (Cause & Discharge) lists being under the influence / drug-policy violations as conduct that can result in **immediate termination**, with no reference to the DATWA first-positive rehabilitation requirement or testing safeguards.

**The gap / opportunity:** Write the statutory "second chance" and testing safeguards directly into the discipline article so a first-positive discharge is grievable as a contract violation, not just a statutory one.

**Proposed language (new paragraph under Section 6.B):**

> **Drug and Alcohol Testing Safeguards.** Any drug, alcohol, or cannabis testing shall comply with the Minnesota Drug and Alcohol Testing in the Workplace Act (Minn. Stat. §§ 181.950–181.957). Consistent with § 181.953, the Company shall not discharge or discipline an employee on the basis of a first positive confirmatory test result without first giving the employee the opportunity to participate in and complete an appropriate counseling or rehabilitation program. No adverse action shall be taken on an initial screening test that has not been verified by a confirmatory test, and the employee retains the right to explain the result and to an independent confirmatory retest.

## 5. Off-duty lawful cannabis use

**MN law (Minn. Stat. § 181.938 + 2023 cannabis act):** Employers generally **may not refuse to hire, discipline, or discharge** an employee for **off-duty, off-premises use of lawful consumable products, including cannabis**. The exception is **safety-sensitive positions** (defined in § 181.950, subd. 13) and CDL/driving positions, where impairment would threaten health or safety. Employers may always act on **on-the-job** use, possession, or impairment.

**Contract today:** Section 6.B prohibits "being under the influence of alcohol or controlled substances … while working, while on Company premises … or while using Company machinery." It does **not** address legal off-duty cannabis use, leaving members exposed to discipline that may violate § 181.938.

**The gap / opportunity:** Clarify that lawful off-duty use is protected and limit testing/discipline to on-duty impairment and genuine safety-sensitive roles. Bargain over **which** classifications are designated "safety-sensitive" so the list isn't drawn unilaterally.

**Proposed language (new paragraph under Section 6.B):**

> **Off-Duty Lawful Use.** Consistent with Minn. Stat. § 181.938, the Company shall not refuse to hire, discipline, or discharge any employee for the use or enjoyment of lawful consumable products, including cannabis, when that use occurs off the Company's premises during nonworking hours. This protection does not apply to use, possession, or impairment during working hours, on Company premises, or while operating Company vehicles, machinery, or equipment, and does not apply to bona fide safety-sensitive positions as defined in Minn. Stat. § 181.950, subd. 13. The specific positions designated safety-sensitive shall be identified by mutual agreement between the Company and the Union.

## 6. Short statutory leaves not in the contract

The contract covers FMLA, jury duty, bereavement, and personal leave, but omits several MN-mandated leaves. Adding them makes them grievable and lets you bargain "paid" where the statute is silent or already requires pay.

| Leave | MN statute | What the law requires | In contract? |
|---|---|---|---|
| **School conference / activities** | § 181.9412 | Up to **16 hrs/yr** (unpaid; employee may use PTO) to attend a child's school conferences/activities | ❌ No |
| **Bone marrow donation** | § 181.945 | Up to **40 work hours PAID** (employers with 20+ employees) | ❌ No |
| **Organ donation** | § 181.9456 | Up to **40 work hours PAID** per donation (employers with 20+ employees) | ❌ No |
| **Voting leave** | Minn. Stat. § 204C.04 | **PAID** time off to vote in covered elections; no points/penalty | ❌ No |

**Proposed language (new subsections under Section 5.F — Leaves of Absence):**

> **School Conference and Activities Leave.** Consistent with Minn. Stat. § 181.9412, the Company shall grant up to sixteen (16) hours of leave in any twelve-month period to attend a child's school conferences or school-related activities that cannot be scheduled during nonwork hours. Employees may use accrued Personal Time or Vacation for this leave. No attendance points shall be assessed.
>
> **Bone Marrow and Organ Donation Leave.** Consistent with Minn. Stat. §§ 181.945 and 181.9456, the Company shall grant **paid** leave of up to forty (40) work hours to an employee donating bone marrow, and up to forty (40) work hours per donation to an employee donating an organ. No attendance points shall be assessed.
>
> **Voting Leave.** Consistent with Minn. Stat. § 204C.04, employees shall be granted **paid** time off as needed to vote in any election covered by the statute. No attendance points shall be assessed.

> 💡 Note: bone-marrow and organ-donation leave are **already point-free** under the law and should be added to the "point-free absences" list in **Section 5.E** alongside ESST, jury duty, etc.

## 7. Personnel record access (Personnel Record Review Act)

**MN law (Minn. Stat. §§ 181.960–181.966):** Employees may review their **personnel file once every 6 months** (former employees once per year), with the employer providing access within **7 working days**. As of **July 1, 2024**, employees also have the right to **dispute and correct** information in the file. This is valuable evidence-gathering for grievances and discipline challenges.

**Contract today:** No reference. Section 1.B mentions the Company *retains* records but says nothing about member access.

**Proposed language (new paragraph, suggested under Section 6.B or Shop Rules):**

> **Personnel File Access.** Consistent with Minn. Stat. §§ 181.960–181.966, an employee or the employee's Union representative (with written authorization) may review the employee's personnel record, and the Company shall provide access within seven (7) working days of a written request. The employee has the right to submit a written statement disputing any information in the file, which shall be maintained with the record. A copy of any disciplinary document shall be provided to the employee and, upon request, to the Union Steward.

## 8. Wage disclosure protection

**MN law (Minn. Stat. § 181.172, part of WESA):** Employers **may not prohibit or retaliate against** employees for **disclosing or discussing their own wages.** Many handbook/confidentiality rules conflict with this.

**Contract today:** Section 6.B lists "Disclosure of confidential information" as terminable conduct — broad enough to be read as chilling wage discussions, which would violate both § 181.172 and the NLRA.

**The gap / opportunity:** Carve out wage discussions explicitly so the confidentiality rule can't be used against members talking pay (relevant given the published wage scale in Exhibit C).

**Proposed language (clarifying note to Section 6.B "Disclosure of confidential information"):**

> Nothing in this Agreement or any Company policy shall be construed to prohibit employees from disclosing or discussing their own wages, hours, or other terms and conditions of employment, consistent with Minn. Stat. § 181.172 and the National Labor Relations Act.

## 9. ESST — already compliant, but room to bargain above the floor

**MN law (Minn. Stat. §§ 181.9445–181.9448):** Accrual of **1 hour per 30 worked, up to 48 hrs/year**, max carryover/balance **80 hrs**. These are statutory **minimums**.

**Contract today:** Section 5.F.3 mirrors the statute almost exactly (1/30, 48/year cap, 80-hr balance) and even includes a clause that it will be amended by LOU to stay compliant. **This is good — the contract is compliant.**

**The gap / opportunity:** Nothing is *missing* legally, but everything here is a floor. The union could bargain a **higher accrual cap** (e.g., 80 hrs/year), a **higher balance cap**, **front-loading** instead of accrual, or **payout of unused ESST at separation** (the contract currently says no payout — Section 5.F.3 — which the law permits but does not require). Flag as an economic ask, not a compliance gap.

## 10. Rest & meal breaks — contract now falls BELOW the law (effective Jan 1, 2026) ⭐

**MN law (Minn. Stat. §§ 177.253, 177.254, amended June 2025):** As of **January 1, 2026**, employers must provide a **paid rest break of at least 15 minutes** (or enough time to use the nearest restroom, whichever is longer) for **every 4 consecutive hours worked**, and an **unpaid 30-minute meal break for shifts of 6+ consecutive hours**. Non-compliance creates liability for the break time at the regular rate **plus an equal amount in liquidated damages**.

**Contract today:** Section 5.B.1 sets breaks at **10 minutes** (one for 4–7 hrs, two for 7–10 hrs, and one 10-min + one 15-min for 10+ hrs). The 30-minute lunch in Section 5.B.2 already meets the meal standard, **but the 10-minute rest breaks are now below the statutory minimum.**

**The gap / opportunity:** This is both a **compliance problem** (members are entitled to 15-minute breaks now, regardless of what the CBA says) and a clean bargaining win — update the contract table to match/exceed the law so it's grievable.

**Proposed language (replace the break-length chart in Section 5.B.1):**

> Employees shall receive one paid rest break of at least fifteen (15) minutes, or enough time to use the nearest convenient restroom (whichever is longer), for every four (4) consecutive hours worked, consistent with Minn. Stat. § 177.253. An unpaid meal break of at least thirty (30) minutes shall be provided for any shift of six (6) or more consecutive hours, consistent with Minn. Stat. § 177.254.

## 11. Broader anti-discrimination — MN Human Rights Act + CROWN Act

**MN law (Minn. Stat. ch. 363A):** The Minnesota Human Rights Act prohibits employment discrimination on **more grounds than federal law**, including sexual orientation, gender identity, marital status, familial status, status with regard to public assistance, and (via the **CROWN Act**) **natural hair texture and protective styles** as part of "race."

**Contract today:** Section 1.B only bars discrimination "because of membership or activity in the Union." There is **no general non-discrimination / anti-harassment clause** tied to protected classes, and no contractual route to grieve discrimination.

**The gap / opportunity:** A contractual non-discrimination clause lets members grieve discrimination through the CBA (and to arbitration) instead of relying solely on an MDHR/EEOC charge.

**Proposed language (new paragraph under Section 1.B or a new "Non-Discrimination" article):**

> **Non-Discrimination.** The Company shall not discriminate against or harass any employee on any basis protected by the Minnesota Human Rights Act (Minn. Stat. ch. 363A) or federal law, including race (and traits associated with race such as hair texture and protective hairstyles), color, creed, religion, national origin, sex, sexual orientation, gender identity, marital status, familial status, disability, age, status with regard to public assistance, or union activity. Alleged violations shall be subject to the grievance and arbitration procedure.

## 12. Final paycheck timing, whistleblower & crime-victim protections

**MN law:**
- **§§ 181.13 / 181.14 (final pay):** A **discharged** employee's earned wages are due **immediately** (within 24 hours of demand); an employee who **quits** is paid by the next regular payday. Late payment can trigger penalty wages.
- **§ 181.932 (Whistleblower Act):** No retaliation for a good-faith report of an actual/suspected/planned legal violation, or for refusing an order the employee believes is illegal.
- **§ 611A.036 (crime-victim leave):** Reasonable time off (and no retaliation) for a victim of violent crime — and the victim's spouse/immediate family — to attend related criminal proceedings.

**Contract today:** None of these appear. The discipline article (Section 6.B) does not reference whistleblower protection, and there's no final-pay-timing or crime-victim-leave language.

**Proposed language (new short subsections):**

> **Final Pay.** Upon discharge, all wages earned shall be paid consistent with Minn. Stat. § 181.13; upon voluntary separation, by the next regularly scheduled payday consistent with Minn. Stat. § 181.14.
>
> **Whistleblower Protection.** Consistent with Minn. Stat. § 181.932, no employee shall be disciplined or retaliated against for a good-faith report of a violation of law or for refusing to perform an action the employee reasonably believes is unlawful. Such retaliation is grievable.
>
> **Crime-Victim Leave.** Consistent with Minn. Stat. § 611A.036, employees who are victims of a violent crime, and their qualifying family members, shall be granted reasonable time off to attend related criminal proceedings without retaliation. No attendance points shall be assessed.

## 13. Federal-law floor worth locking into the contract

Several federal laws give protections that aren't spelled out in the CBA. Even where MN law overlaps, writing them in makes them grievable and clarifies which standard (state or federal — whichever is more generous) applies.

| Federal law | What it provides | In contract? |
|---|---|---|
| **Pregnant Workers Fairness Act (PWFA)** | Reasonable accommodation for pregnancy/childbirth/related conditions (employers 15+); same framework as ADA | ❌ (see #3) |
| **PUMP Act** | Reasonable break time + private space to express milk for 1 year (FLSA) | ❌ (see #2) |
| **ADA** | Reasonable accommodation / interactive process for disabilities | ❌ No accommodation clause |
| **USERRA** | Military leave + reemployment, seniority, benefit continuation | Mentioned only in passing (COBRA/USERRA reference in FMLA section) |
| **WARN Act** | 60 days' advance notice of mass layoff/plant closing (employers 100+), notice to the Union | ❌ Contract only gives **3 working days** layoff notice (Sec. 6.D) |
| **NLRA § 7 / Weingarten** | Right to representation in investigatory interviews; protected concerted activity | Partially — Sec. 1.B has a Weingarten-style rep right |

**Proposed language (new "Reasonable Accommodation" subsection, and a WARN cross-reference in Section 6.D):**

> **Reasonable Accommodation.** The Company shall provide reasonable accommodation to qualified employees with disabilities (ADA) and for pregnancy, childbirth, and related conditions (PWFA / Minn. Stat. § 181.9414), engaging in a timely, good-faith interactive process. Denials are subject to the grievance procedure.
>
> **Advance Notice (add to Section 6.D – Layoff).** In the event of a plant closing or mass layoff covered by the federal WARN Act, the Company shall provide all notice required by that Act to affected employees and to the Union. [Bargaining goal: extend the contract's standard layoff notice beyond three (3) working days.]
