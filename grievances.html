⚖️ GRIEVANCE FAQs — TAP TO EXPAND

A grievance is a formal complaint when the employer violates the contract. Members have the right to file one. Contact the union steward first — the steward guides members through the steps.
There are 4 contract steps. Step 1: employee and/or steward meets with management (48 hours). Step 2: steward + business rep + management (10 working days). Step 3: FMCS mediation (within 30 calendar days of notice to arbitrate). Step 4: binding arbitration.
If called into an HR or supervisor meeting that could lead to discipline, request the union steward be present. Do not sign statements, waivers, or "last chance" papers until speaking with the steward.
No. Retaliation for filing a grievance is illegal. The NLRA protects the right to engage in union activity. Document any retaliation and contact the steward immediately.
Ask payroll first for a written correction. If the company does not fix it quickly (or by next check), contact the union or steward and file a grievance within contract deadlines. Keep pay stubs, screenshots, and dates.
Overtime must be paid per contract (typically 1.5x after 8 hrs/day or 40 hrs/week). Unpaid overtime is wage theft — file a grievance and contact MN Dept of Labor.
Yes. Workers have the right to refuse unsafe work under OSHA. Report hazards to the steward and safety committee. See the Safety page for more.
Seniority rules are in the contract. If layoffs, promotions, or job assignments violate seniority, file a grievance. Time limits apply — act quickly.
Some steps require written grievances. The steward helps draft them. Include date, facts, contract section violated, and remedy requested.
Grievances must usually be filed within a set number of days (often 5–10) of when the member knew or should have known of the violation. Check the contract — delays can waive rights.
If hazardous fume exposure is suspected (e.g., welding fumes, paint overspray, grinding dust), document it immediately. Report to the steward and request a safety review. OSHA requires ventilation and PPE. See contract and Safety page for PPE requirements.
This contract text does not list a tool allowance. It does mention uniforms through payroll deduction. If management requires boots, tools, or other gear, ask HR for the written policy and then contact the steward about reimbursement or grievance options.
FMLA is unpaid, job-protected federal leave. Minnesota Paid Leave (PFML) is paid state leave that started in 2026. Eligible workers can receive up to 12 weeks medical leave, up to 12 weeks family leave, and up to 20 weeks total in one benefit year. Apply through paidleave.mn.gov as soon as leave dates are known.
