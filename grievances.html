⚖️ THE GRIEVANCE PROCESS — 4 STEPS (Contract Article 6C)

STEP 1 — TALK IT OUT
You + Union Steward + Management
Bring the issue to your supervisor with your steward present. Management has 48 hours to respond. If not resolved, your steward will put the grievance in writing within 3 days.
STEP 2 — ESCALATE
Union Steward + Business Rep + Company President
If Step 1 didn't fix it, the Union Business Rep gets involved and meets with company leadership. They have 10 working days to work it out.
STEP 3 — MEDIATION
Federal Mediator (FMCS)
A neutral federal mediator from the Federal Mediation & Conciliation Service helps both sides reach agreement. Must happen within 30 calendar days of the notice to arbitrate.
STEP 4 — BINDING ARBITRATION
Independent Arbitrator Makes Final Decision
If mediation fails, an arbitrator is selected from a list of 5 provided by the State. Their decision is final and binding. Each side pays their own costs; arbitrator fees are split 50/50.

⚖️ GRIEVANCE FAQs — TAP TO EXPAND

A grievance is a formal complaint when the employer violates the contract. You have the right to file one. Contact your steward first — they guide you through the steps.
There are 4 steps defined in the contract (Article 6C):

Step 1: You and your steward meet with management. They have 48 hours to respond. If unresolved, it goes to writing within 3 days.
Step 2: The Union Business Rep meets with company leadership. They have 10 working days to resolve it.
Step 3: A neutral federal mediator (FMCS) helps both sides. Must happen within 30 days.
Step 4: Binding arbitration — an independent arbitrator makes the final call.

Most grievances are settled at Step 1 or 2. Your steward handles the paperwork and guides you through it.
You have the right to union representation (Weingarten rights). Say: "I want my steward present." Do not sign anything until you talk to your steward.

Report wage errors to your steward right away. Keep pay stubs and records. Check your rate against the contract wage scale (Exhibit C) — see the Benefits page pay estimator. Wage theft is a grievance. You can also report to MN DLI: 651-284-5075.
Per contract (Article 5C): 1.5x for hours beyond your normal shift or normal work week. 2x for 7th day or hours over 60. 3x on holidays (holiday pay + 2x hours worked). Unpaid overtime is wage theft — file a grievance and report to MN Dept of Labor & Industry: 651-284-5075.
Yes. You also have the right to refuse unsafe work under OSHA. Report hazards to your steward and safety committee. See the Safety page for more.
Seniority rules are in the contract. If layoffs, promotions, or job assignments violate seniority, file a grievance. Time limits apply — act quickly.
Some steps require written grievances. Your steward will help you draft it. Include date, facts, contract section violated, and remedy requested.
Act fast. In Step 1, management has 48 hours to respond. If unresolved, the grievance must be put in writing within 3 days. Step 2 has a 10 working day window. For discharge challenges, you must notify the company in writing within 5 business days (Article 6B). Delays can waive your rights — tell your steward immediately when something happens.
If you suspect hazardous fume exposure (e.g., welding fumes, paint overspray, grinding dust), document it immediately. Report to your steward and request a safety review. OSHA requires ventilation and PPE. See contract and Safety page for PPE requirements.
The contract (Article 8A) allows payroll deduction for position-required equipment and material purchases over $250, available to employees with 1+ year of seniority. The employer must provide all required PPE (safety glasses, hearing protection, respirators) under OSHA law — you should not have to pay for PPE. If you're being asked to buy your own PPE, that's a potential grievance. Talk to your steward.
FMLA (federal) = job-protected unpaid leave — up to 12 weeks for serious health conditions, family care, military family leave.

PFML (MN state, effective Jan 1, 2026) = paid leave — up to 12 weeks medical, 12 weeks family, or 20 weeks combined. Pay is 90% of wages up to $711.50/week, then 66%, then 55%. Max $1,423/week. Apply at paidleave.mn.gov.

You can use both at the same time. Your contract also has short-term disability (employee-paid). Ask your Union Steward how all three work together. See the Benefits page for full details.
