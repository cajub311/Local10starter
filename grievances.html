⚖️ GRIEVANCE FAQs — TAP TO EXPAND

A grievance is a formal complaint when the employer violates the contract. You have the right to file one. Contact your steward first — they guide you through the steps.
Typically 3–4 steps: (1) Steward & supervisor, (2) Business rep & management, (3) Joint committee, (4) Arbitration. See contract § for exact procedure.
You have the right to union representation (Weingarten rights). Say: "I want my steward present." Do not sign anything until you talk to your steward.
No. Retaliation for filing a grievance is illegal. The NLRA protects your right to engage in union activity. Document any retaliation and contact your steward immediately.
Report wage errors to your steward within the timeframe in your contract (often 5–10 days). Keep pay stubs and records. Wage theft is a grievance.
Overtime must be paid per contract (typically 1.5x after 8 hrs/day or 40 hrs/week). Unpaid overtime is wage theft — file a grievance and contact MN Dept of Labor.
Yes. You also have the right to refuse unsafe work under OSHA. Report hazards to your steward and safety committee. See the Safety page for more.
Seniority rules are in the contract. If layoffs, promotions, or job assignments violate seniority, file a grievance. Time limits apply — act quickly.
Some steps require written grievances. Your steward will help you draft it. Include date, facts, contract section violated, and remedy requested.
Contact your steward anyway. They can advise whether it's covered by the contract. When in doubt, ask — that's what the union is for.
For safety violations, you may report to OSHA anonymously. For grievances, your steward can discuss options. MN law protects whistleblowers from retaliation.
Grievances must usually be filed within a set number of days (often 5–10) of when you knew or should have known of the violation. Check your contract — delays can waive your rights.
If you suspect hazardous fume exposure (e.g., welding fumes, paint overspray, grinding dust), document it immediately. Report to your steward and request a safety review. OSHA requires ventilation and PPE. See contract and Safety page for PPE requirements.
If your contract provides a tool allowance and the employer isn't paying it, that's a grievance. Check your contract for the exact amount and timing. Keep records of what you've spent. Report to your steward.
If you spot non-union workers performing sheet metal work on a job site, don't confront anyone. Document date, time, location, company name, and report to your steward. The union may pursue contract violations. See Safety page for the report form.
