CONTRACT vs LAW

Crosswalk of the Atlas Manufacturing / SMART Local 10 CBA (Apr 1, 2025 – Mar 31, 2027) against Minnesota statutes and Minneapolis ordinances. The contract PDF/text is not changed. This page only explains what the CBA covers, what law still stacks on top, and gaps to watch.

Unofficial steward tool · Not legal advice · Not affiliated with SMART Local 10 · Review dates Jul 2026

How to read this

In CBA = contract language exists · Partial = something in CBA but weaker/missing pieces · Gap = not in CBA — use statute/ordinance + grievance strategy · Law floor = law always applies even if CBA is silent

Workers always get the better of CBA vs law (the higher floor). CBA cannot undercut most statutory rights.

Biggest gaps for this shop

CBA GAP Subcontracting / outsourcing

The CBA does not clearly ban or limit sending unit work (e.g. powder coating / paint) to outside contractors. Art. 2 limits temps and supervisors displacing union work — that is not the same as a full subcontract clause.

What you can still do: demand notice; bargain effects (transfers, rate retention, overtime opportunity, layoff order); file if temps exceed 10% or displace OT; document paint work leaving the plant while painters are reassigned.

NLRA duty to bargain effects of subcontracting · UE/steward practice: effects bargaining even when decision language is weak

CBA PARTIAL Painter → Assembly reassignment

Exhibit C has both Painter and Assembler rates. Art. 4 limits long stints in a higher class without remedy — it does not clearly freeze you in Painter if management reassigns within the unit.

Watch: rate if moved (must get correct classification pay); OT rights; whether work is “normally performed” in another class; whether temps/outside shops take the paint while you assemble.

MINNEAPOLIS City floors (plant on Weeks Ave SE)

Atlas is in Minneapolis. City ordinances stack on top of the CBA:

• Minimum wage — city rate for employees working ≥2 hours/week in Minneapolis (check current poster; rates rise each year; 2026 city MW is higher than state MW).
• Earned Sick & Safe Time (city) — must follow the rule most favorable to workers (city + state). Statewide ESST already in CBA.
• Required posters — MW + sick time poster in languages employees use.

minimumwage.minneapolismn.gov · sicktimeinfo.minneapolismn.gov · dli.mn.gov/sick-leave

MINNESOTA Laws not written into the CBA (but still bind the employer)

• Wage theft & pay notices — Minn. Stat. 181.032, 609.52
• Final wages — 181.13 (discharge) / 181.14 (quit)
• Personnel file access — 181.960–.966
• Drug/alcohol testing limits — 181.950–.957
• Whistleblower — 181.932
• Pregnancy & nursing — 181.939
• Wage disclosure — 181.172 (talking pay is protected)
• Paid Leave (PFML) — benefits begin 2026 (see Benefits page)
• Workers' comp — report injury same day; written notice rules (176.141)

These do not need to be in the CBA to be enforceable. Use them with grievances or DLI/MDHR/NLRB as needed.

FEDERAL Always available

NLRA §7 concerted activity · Weingarten · OSHA / MNOSHA · FLSA overtime · FMLA (job protection) · Title VII / ADA / ADEA · NLRA unfair labor practices for anti-union retaliation.

Ideas for next negotiations (not in current CBA)

These are bargaining goals, not current rights. Only a ratified contract changes them:

1. Subcontracting clause — notice, no-subcontract of core paint/production, or preferential recall of work.
2. Classification retention / rate red circle when reassigned for employer convenience.
3. Successorship if company sells.
4. Stronger layoff notice (beyond 3 days) for big cuts.
5. Explicit PFML coordination language.
6. Heat / chemical (paint booth) joint safety language beyond PPE lists.
