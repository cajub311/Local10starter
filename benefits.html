🧾 Paystub Decoder — What Are All These Deductions?

Tap any line on your stub below to see what it's for and who to call with questions.

UNION DUESLocal 10 Dues Check-Off
Automatically deducted per the CBA. Typically a small percentage of gross or a fixed amount. Funds your contract, rep, steward, grievance defense, organizing. Questions: call the Union Hall.
H&WHealth & Welfare
Employer contribution to your health plan — usually shown for transparency, not taken from your check. If you see it subtracted, ask payroll. Claims: 651-646-2650.
PENSIONDefined Benefit Pension
Employer contribution per hours worked, credited to your pension account. Not taxed now — taxed when drawn in retirement. Tracks toward vesting (typically 5 yrs).
ANNUITY / 401(k)Retirement Annuity
Additional retirement savings — some of this may be your optional contribution. Check with fund office before changing your deferral rate.
PFMLMN Paid Family & Medical Leave
New for 2026. Split between you and employer. Qualifies you for up to 20 weeks of partial-pay leave for your own serious illness or family care. State-run program.
FED / STATE / FICATaxes
Federal + MN state income tax (based on W-4), plus Social Security (6.2%) and Medicare (1.45%). Update W-4 through HR if your family status changed.
MISC DEDUCTAnything Unfamiliar?
If you see a deduction you didn't authorize, flag it immediately. Take a photo of your stub and text your steward: 612-428-8004. Employer cannot deduct without authorization.

👨‍👩‍👧‍👦 MN Paid Family & Medical Leave (PFML) — 2026

Paid Leave is Minnesota's state program for your own serious health condition or family caregiving needs.

  • ✅ Starts: Jan. 1, 2026
  • ✅ Length: Up to 12 weeks medical leave, up to 12 weeks family leave, up to 20 weeks total in one benefit year
  • ✅ Pay: Partial wage replacement based on income (state formula)
  • ✅ Job protection: Return to same or equivalent position when leave ends
  • ✅ How to apply: Apply at paidleave.mn.gov as soon as you know your leave dates
  • ✅ PFML vs short-term disability: STD usually covers only your own condition; PFML also covers family leave. Ask HR how benefits coordinate so you avoid delays or overpayments.
  • ⚠️ If HR denies or delays leave/pay, contact your steward immediately.
⚖️ MN & Federal Law — Rights Beyond Our Contract

📌 These are NOT in our current contract — and this page does not change it.

Minnesota and federal law give workers rights on top of the CBA. Some you already have right now — even though they're not written in our contract — so know them and use them. Others are goals to bring to the table when we renegotiate. The contract stays exactly as-is until the Union and Company bargain a new one.

🟢 Rights you already have by law — use them now
NEW 2026Longer PAID breaks — 15 min per 4 hours
Effective Jan 1, 2026, MN law requires a paid rest break of at least 15 minutes (or enough time to reach the nearest restroom, whichever is longer) for every 4 consecutive hours worked. Our contract still lists 10-minute breaks (Sec. 5.B) — that's now below the legal minimum. You're entitled to the longer break. Flag short-staffing or denied breaks to your steward. Minn. Stat. § 177.253
MEALS30-min meal break on 6+ hour shifts
As of Jan 1, 2026, an unpaid 30-minute meal break is required for shifts of 6+ consecutive hours. Our 30-minute lunch already meets this. Minn. Stat. § 177.254
NURSINGPaid lactation breaks — no time limit
Reasonable paid break time each day to express milk, with no 12-month cutoff and no "it disrupts work" excuse. A private space (not a bathroom) must be provided. Pay can't be reduced for this time. Minn. Stat. § 181.939 · federal PUMP Act
PREGNANCYPregnancy accommodations — no doctor's note needed
On request: more frequent/longer breaks, a seat, a 20-lb lifting limit, and temporary transfer to lighter work — no medical documentation required. Plus up to 12 weeks of job-protected pregnancy/parental leave with continued health coverage. Minn. Stat. §§ 181.9414, 181.941 · federal PWFA
CANNABISOff-duty legal cannabis is protected
You generally can't be fired, disciplined, or refused hire for legal cannabis/consumable use off the clock and off premises. Exceptions: on-the-job use/impairment and bona fide safety-sensitive jobs (e.g., CDL). Minn. Stat. § 181.938
DRUG TESTSecond chance on a first positive test
Under MN's DATWA, the company can't fire you on a first positive confirmatory test without first offering you a counseling/rehab program. You also have the right to an explanation and a confirmatory retest. If they skip this, call your steward. Minn. Stat. § 181.953
FILESee & dispute your personnel file
You can review your personnel file (once every 6 months), and the company must provide it within 7 working days. Since July 2024 you can also submit a written statement disputing anything in it. Great evidence for a grievance. Minn. Stat. §§ 181.960–181.966
PAY TALKYou can discuss your pay
No employer rule can stop you from disclosing or discussing your own wages, and they can't retaliate for it. The "confidential information" rule doesn't override this. Minn. Stat. § 181.172 · NLRA
FINAL PAYFast final paycheck
If you're fired, wages earned are due immediately (within 24 hours of demand). If you quit, by the next regular payday. Minn. Stat. §§ 181.13, 181.14
VOTINGPaid time off to vote
You get paid time off as needed to vote in covered elections — no penalty, no points. Minn. Stat. § 204C.04
DONATIONPaid bone marrow / organ donation leave
Up to 40 paid work hours to donate bone marrow, and up to 40 paid hours per organ donation (employers with 20+ employees). Minn. Stat. §§ 181.945, 181.9456
SCHOOLSchool conference / activities leave
Up to 16 hours per year to attend your child's school conferences or activities that can't be scheduled outside work. You can use PTO for it. Minn. Stat. § 181.9412
RIGHTSWhistleblower & crime-victim protections
You can't be retaliated against for a good-faith report of an illegal practice (or refusing to break the law). Victims of violent crime (and close family) also get reasonable time off to attend criminal proceedings. Minn. Stat. §§ 181.932, 611A.036
EQUALITYBroad anti-discrimination + CROWN Act
The MN Human Rights Act bars discrimination on more grounds than federal law — including sexual orientation, gender identity, marital/familial status, and public-assistance status — and the CROWN Act protects natural hair texture and styles as part of race. Minn. Stat. ch. 363A
LAYOFFAdvance notice of mass layoff / closing
The federal WARN Act requires 60 days' advance notice of a plant closing or mass layoff at larger employers, with notice to your Union too. Worth knowing alongside the contract's layoff/recall language. Federal WARN Act · MN dislocated-worker law
HIRINGPay transparency & salary-history ban
MN job postings (employers with 30+) must list a salary range and a description of benefits, and employers can't ask about your past pay. Useful when posting/bidding internal jobs. Minn. Stat. §§ 181.173, 181.172
🎯 Bargaining goals — bring these to the next contract
  • 💰 Employer pays 100% of the PFML premium. The law only requires the company to cover half — bargaining the rest is a raise (no deduction from your check).
  • 💵 Top-up leave to full pay. PFML and other leaves only replace part of your wages — negotiate the right to top up with Vacation/Personal/ESST, or a company supplement.
  • 📈 Keep seniority & vacation accruing and health at active-employee rates during PFML/FMLA (our FMLA section currently suspends accrual).
  • 🚫 No attendance points for any protected leave (PFML, jury, donation, voting, school, etc.).
  • ⏱️ Write the new 15-minute breaks into the contract so they're grievable, not just statutory.
  • 🤒 ESST above the legal floor — higher accrual cap, or payout of unused time at separation (current contract pays nothing out).
  • 📢 Longer layoff notice than the current 3 working days, and joint say over which jobs are labeled "safety-sensitive."
